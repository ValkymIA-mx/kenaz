## Recruit and prepare

Recruit about five people matching the intended audience: everyday users who reuse passwords, have not set up two-step login, or received a YubiKey and do not know how to begin. Represent both Spanish and English; recruit neither project members nor security-minded friends. Record each participant's preferred language without assuming equal language fluency. Obtain consent to take notes; do not collect real passwords, PINs, recovery codes or account information.

Print the participant's language version, cut or copy H1–H10 into separate cards, and keep H11 and the journey map as facilitator references. Prepare two distinct paper key tokens, a one-port marker, browser and printer cards, and blank paper for hand-written codes. The facilitator simulates site responses and changes cards; nothing is actually clicked, registered, printed, or written to hardware. Provide only the scenario and neutral prompts; do not explain the intended answer. Ask participants to think aloud, allow pauses, and note interventions separately.

## Tasks

1. **Home:** “You want to make your email account safer. What would you do first?” Observe where they start and what they expect to happen.
2. **W1 with two keys:** “You have two keys and one USB port. Get ready to protect an account.” Show cards according to choices. Observe plug, unplug, swap and any touch; ask what they expect each key to do and where the spare belongs.
3. **W2:** “Protect your email.” Use a hypothetical site supporting key sign-in. The facilitator plays the site and confirms each registration only after the participant takes the corresponding step. Observe browser handoffs, independent registration, returning to the app and site backup-code handling. If the participant skips the spare, show the pending warning. On a separate pass, use a code-only hypothetical site and observe QR/link, encrypted save, key swap and printer handoffs without real secrets.
4. **Comprehension, without coaching:** “In your own words, why are there two keys? What is on each one? What happens if you lose the main key? Does adding one key on a website automatically add the other? What would you do with the site's backup codes?” Record actual answers, including uncertainty; do not grade an unasked question as passed.
5. **Optional non-happy branch:** show a missing spare, a full key, or interrupted save and ask what the person thinks happened and what they would do next. Do not imply that all branches or W6 have been validated by this round.

Close by asking what was confusing and thanking the participant. Do not coach during the task; if blocked, record the blocking point and exact neutral or substantive help before proceeding.

## Synthesis and gate

After sessions, group observed issues by screen and language, distinguish direct observations from interpretations, and retain disconfirming evidence. Fix every issue that stops a participant and re-check it with participants before closing U0; smaller issues feed the next milestone's wireframes. Record changes and re-check outcomes here when they exist. Until then, U0 is not run and the M1 wireframe gate is not met.
